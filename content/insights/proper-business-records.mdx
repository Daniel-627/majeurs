Good record-keeping isn't just a compliance requirement — it's the difference between knowing how your business is actually doing and guessing.

## What to keep

At minimum, every business should retain:

- **Sales records** — invoices or receipts for everything you sell
- **Purchase records** — receipts for stock, supplies and expenses
- **Bank statements** — for every business account
- **Payroll records** — if you have employees
- **Asset records** — equipment, vehicles, anything of lasting value to the business

## How long to keep them

KRA generally expects businesses to retain records for a minimum of five years, since that's within the window they can audit. Keep both physical and digital copies where possible — a single lost receipt rarely matters, but a lost year of records is a real problem.

## Habits that make this easy

1. **Separate business and personal accounts.** Mixing the two is the single biggest cause of messy books.
2. **Reconcile monthly, not annually.** Catching a discrepancy within weeks is a five-minute fix. Catching it a year later can take days.
3. **Digitize as you go.** A photo of a receipt taken the same day beats a shoebox of paper six months later.
4. **Use one system consistently** — whether that's a simple spreadsheet or proper accounting software, consistency matters more than sophistication.

## Why this matters beyond tax

Clean records are what make a loan application, an investor conversation, or a sale of the business possible. Nobody can evaluate a business they can't see clearly.
