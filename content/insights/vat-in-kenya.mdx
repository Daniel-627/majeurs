Value Added Tax (VAT) is a tax on the sale of most goods and services in Kenya, collected by registered businesses on behalf of KRA.

## Who needs to register

Any business with annual taxable turnover above the VAT registration threshold is legally required to register. Businesses below the threshold can register voluntarily, which is sometimes useful if most of your customers are VAT-registered businesses themselves, since they'll want to reclaim the VAT you charge them.

## The standard rate

Kenya's standard VAT rate is **16%**, applied to most goods and services. A smaller set of goods and services are zero-rated or exempt — the distinction matters, since zero-rated supplies still let you reclaim input VAT, while exempt ones don't.

## Output VAT vs. input VAT

- **Output VAT** is what you charge your customers on sales.
- **Input VAT** is what you've paid on business purchases and expenses.

What you remit to KRA each period is the difference between the two — output VAT collected minus input VAT paid.

## Filing

VAT returns are filed monthly, even in months with no sales — a "nil return" still needs to be submitted. Missing a filing deadline attracts penalties regardless of whether you owe tax that month.

## A quick example

If you sell goods for KES 100,000 (excluding VAT), you charge your customer KES 16,000 in VAT, for a total of KES 116,000. If you'd paid KES 6,000 in VAT on your own purchases that month, you'd remit the difference — KES 10,000 — to KRA.
