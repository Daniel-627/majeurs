Whether it's a statutory audit, a lender-requested one, or a KRA review, preparation makes the biggest difference in how smoothly it goes.

## Start with your records, not your explanations

Auditors work from documents, not conversations. Before anything else, make sure you can produce:

- Bank statements for the full period under review
- Sales and purchase invoices, matched to your books
- Payroll records, if applicable
- Prior year financial statements, for comparison

If a document doesn't exist or can't be found, that's worth knowing *before* the audit starts, not during it.

## Reconcile before you're asked to

Don't wait for the auditor to flag a discrepancy between your bank statement and your books — find and resolve it yourself first. A business that walks in with clean reconciliations signals good controls; one that's still reconciling mid-audit signals the opposite.

## Know your own numbers

Be ready to explain any unusual swings — a quarter with unusually high expenses, a drop in revenue, a large one-off transaction. Auditors will ask about anomalies; having the explanation ready (and documented) speeds things up considerably.

## Designate one point of contact

Audits move faster when there's a single person who can answer questions or quickly find who can, rather than requests bouncing between departments or getting lost.

## What good preparation actually saves you

A well-prepared audit is shorter, costs less if you're paying by the hour, and rarely turns up surprises — because the surprises get caught during your own preparation instead.
