Most tax problems we see aren't dishonesty — they're avoidable mistakes that compound over time.

## 1. Filing nil returns incorrectly (or not at all)

If your business had no activity in a period, you still need to file a nil return. Skipping it because "there was nothing to report" still triggers penalties.

## 2. Missing deadlines by a few days

Tax penalties and interest typically apply from the day after the deadline, regardless of how close you were. A filing three days late is treated the same as one three months late in terms of triggering a penalty.

## 3. Mixing personal and business expenses

Claiming personal expenses as business costs — or failing to separate the two clearly — is one of the fastest ways to complicate an otherwise straightforward filing, and a common flag in an audit.

## 4. Not reconciling VAT input and output monthly

Waiting until year-end to reconcile VAT means errors have had twelve months to compound, and finding the source of a discrepancy gets much harder the longer it sits.

## 5. Ignoring statutory deduction changes

PAYE bands, NSSF rates, and other statutory figures are set by law and can change. Continuing to run payroll on outdated figures creates underpayment liability that accumulates silently until caught.

## 6. Treating tax as a year-end problem

The businesses with the smoothest tax season are the ones that treat compliance as a monthly habit, not an annual scramble. Waiting until the deadline to figure out what you owe rarely goes well.
