PAYE (Pay As You Earn) is the system through which employers deduct income tax directly from employees' salaries and remit it to KRA, rather than employees paying it themselves at year-end.

## How it's calculated

PAYE is calculated on a graduated scale — the more someone earns, the higher the rate on each additional shilling, not on the whole salary. Kenya's bands run from 10% on the lowest portion of income up to 35% on the highest, with several bands in between.

Every employee is also entitled to a **personal relief** — a fixed monthly amount subtracted from the tax calculated, reducing what's actually payable.

## What employers must do

- Calculate PAYE correctly for every employee, every pay cycle
- Deduct it from gross pay before disbursing salaries
- Remit the deducted amount to KRA by the statutory deadline each month
- Issue accurate payslips showing the deduction

Getting this wrong — whether through miscalculation or late remittance — creates liability for the business, not just the employee.

## Beyond PAYE

Employers also typically need to handle other statutory deductions alongside PAYE — NSSF (pension), SHIF (health insurance), and the Housing Levy. Each has its own rate and remittance deadline, and they're usually processed in the same payroll run as PAYE.

## A practical note

Because tax bands and relief amounts are set by the Finance Act, they can change — sometimes mid-year. A payroll process that hard-codes last year's figures is a common source of underpayment.
