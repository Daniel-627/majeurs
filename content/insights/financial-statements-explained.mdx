Most business owners have seen their financial statements without really reading them. Each of the three core statements answers a different question.

## The Income Statement — "Did we make money?"

Also called the Profit & Loss statement, this covers a period of time (a month, a quarter, a year) and shows revenue, costs, and what's left over. It answers: **was the business profitable in this period?**

It does *not* tell you how much cash you have — a business can show a profit on paper while struggling with cash flow, if customers haven't paid yet.

## The Balance Sheet — "What do we own and owe?"

This is a snapshot at a single point in time, not a period. It lists:

- **Assets** — what the business owns (cash, equipment, money owed to you)
- **Liabilities** — what the business owes (loans, unpaid bills)
- **Equity** — what's left over for the owners once liabilities are subtracted from assets

It answers: **what is the business actually worth right now?**

## The Cash Flow Statement — "Where did the cash go?"

This tracks actual cash moving in and out, split into three categories: operating activities (day-to-day business), investing activities (buying/selling assets), and financing activities (loans, owner contributions).

It answers the question the Income Statement can't: **do we have the cash to cover what's coming?** A profitable business can still run out of cash if it's not managed.

## Why all three matter together

A business owner looking at only one statement gets a partial picture. Profitable but cash-poor, or cash-rich but not actually profitable — both situations look fine on one statement and concerning on another. Reading them together is what gives you the real picture.
